\question Question 4.
Prove that in an arbitry topological space, the union of two closed sets is closed, using the limit point definition of closed sets.
\begin{solution}
    Suppose $A$ and $B$ are closed sets in a topological space $(X, \tau)$. 
    Let $C = A \cup B$. We want to show that $C$ is closed, which means we need to show that $C$ contains all its limit points.
    
    Suppose for contradiction that there exists a limit point $x$ of $C$ such that $x \notin C$. 
    
    Since $x$ is a limit point of $C$, every open neighborhood of $x$ must intersect $C$ in some point other than $x$. 
    
    Since $C = A \cup B$, this means that every open neighborhood of $x$ must intersect either $A$ or $B$ (or both) in some point other than $x$. But since $A$ and $B$ are closed, they contain all their limit points. This is a contradiction.
\end{solution}
