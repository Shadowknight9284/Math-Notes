\question Would the model be one in which we redefine the the laplacian to encode  

the original model Lindgren (2011):
$$ (\kappa^2 - \Delta)^{\alpha/2} Z(x,t) = W(x,t)$$
With $\Delta = \frac{\partial^2}{\partial t^2} + \sum_{i = 1}^d \frac{\partial^2}{\partial x_i^2}$

Note that I am not certain if this is how most spatio-temporal Matern models are characterized, im simply assuming since the time dimension is treated the same as a spatial one.

Into 
$$ (\kappa^2 - D)^{\alpha/2} Z(x,t) = W(x,t)$$
With $D = \frac{\partial}{\partial t} + \sum_{i = 1}^d \frac{\partial^2}{\partial x_i^2}$ 

Or would it be more fitting to have another model in which we have the linear operator
$$ L_x(\kappa, \alpha) = (\kappa^2 - \Delta_x)^
{\alpha/2} \quad \Delta_x = \sum_{x_i \in x} \frac{\partial^2}{\partial x_i^2}$$
and then have the separable model 
$$ L_t(\kappa_t, \alpha_t) L_x(\kappa_x, \alpha_x)Z(x,t) = W(x,t)$$

I think the first characterization is better simply on the pretense we should avoid seperable models, but I am not sure if that intuition holds since I would expect sufficently different behavior looking acros space $\times$ time, as apposed space $\times$ space
